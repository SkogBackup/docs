# @ and $ Symbol Duality Analysis

## Individual Symbol Definitions

### $ (Reference)
- `"$": "to define or reference something"`
- `"$": reference something without being anything - "null, null-pointer"`
- `"$x": $x` - "x is x", "x is a reference to x", "the definition of ID is ID is ID"
- `"$id": $id`, `$a` - "a reference to yourself is yourself", "you are because you are"

### @ (Intent)
- `"@": "the intent to act or do something"`
- `"@": do something to nothing - "void, no return, side-effect"`
- `"@": the intent to act or do something | {$id@$id}`

## Paired Combinations

### @$ Pattern
- `"@$": [=]` - "no transformation allowed | ID is ID is ID"
- **Transformations**: `(@$)` - intent then reference

### $@ Pattern
- `"$@": [!=]`
- **Types**: `($@)` - reference then intent
- `"to follow or continue something via [$@]"`

## Computational Structures
- **Types**: `($@)` - reference then intent
- **Transformations**: `(@$)` - intent then reference  
- **Functions**: `($@=@$)` - bidirectional equivalence

## Fundamental Duality

### Positive vs Negative Space
- `$` operates in **positive space** - identity, what is, having, being
- `@` operates in **negative space** - transformation, what's not yet, missing, becoming
- `@$` and `$@` are the **interfaces** between being and not-being

### Philosophical Context
@ and $ duality represents the fundamental split between transformation/possibility (@) and state/reality ($)

### Category Theory Context
- `@` = monadic binding (computational contexts)
- All computation reduces to just two primitives (`$` and `@`) and their combinations
